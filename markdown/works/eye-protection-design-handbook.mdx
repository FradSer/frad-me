At ByteDance, I helped establish the GIP UED Creative Design Lab and led research for the Eye Protection Design Handbook. My work translated questions about visual comfort into practical guidance, interface examples, and prototypes for product teams, including Douyin, Xigua Video, and Jinri Toutiao.

## Context and Research Question

> Why is it designed this way?

The lab connected design decisions with the reasoning behind them. Teams working across mobile products and larger displays needed a way to discuss viewing distance, text size, color, contrast, and screen use together.

The question was how to make these considerations part of everyday design decisions. A text size that worked on a familiar phone could be a poor starting point for a different device, viewing distance, or audience.

_This case summarizes the design work. The original internal handbook is not publicly available. The calculations below are historical project assumptions and examples; they do not establish a clinical benefit or a universal viewing standard._

## Define the Viewing Context

Long screen sessions can involve discomfort such as tired or dry eyes. For this project, the design task was to examine the reading conditions an interface creates.

Looking at nearby objects involves **accommodation**, which changes the lens's focus, and **convergence**, which turns the eyes inward. These considerations led us to evaluate text size alongside viewing distance, rather than treat either value in isolation.

### Set a Reference Distance

We used **design viewing distance** to describe the distance at which an interface should be evaluated. It combines the viewing task, device size, input method, and practical reach of the user.

For the handheld example in this study, we used **40 cm** as a design reference. It was a project assumption for that example.

We also explored the visual angle of a logical pixel. The model below uses an assumed angle of **1.5/60 degrees** to compare screen size and logical resolution:

### Include Reach and Field of View

We called the distance allowed by the input method the **operable distance**. Touch, a keyboard and mouse, and a remote control each create different constraints. Casting to a TV or projector can separate the controller from the display carrying the content.

We used **presence distance** to describe how the image fills the viewer's field of view. Comparing these distances helped us choose a reference for prototypes and usability tests, particularly on unfamiliar devices.

For the study's iPhone X landscape example, the model produced:

- Reference viewing distance: 38.07 cm.
- Operable distance: 49.50 cm.
- Presence distance: 20.77 cm.

We selected **40 cm** as the design viewing distance for that example.

## Consider the Whole Viewing Environment

The study extended beyond typography to the lighting, transitions, and interruptions around a reading task.

### Offer Configurable Break Reminders

The [American Optometric Association](https://www.aoa.org/healthy-eyes/eye-and-vision-conditions/computer-vision-syndrome) recommends the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds.

For interface design, a reminder should be optional and easy to dismiss, pause, and configure. Its role is to support a routine without creating another obligation.

### Keep Related Surfaces Consistent

A bright screen in a dim room, glare, and reflections can make viewing uncomfortable. We considered the interface alongside the device's brightness controls and its surroundings.

In reading products, we observed abrupt brightness changes when people moved from a dark reading surface to a white page. We explored aligned surface colors and brief transitions to make those changes less disruptive.

Display flicker depends on hardware and brightness settings. Evaluating representative devices is more useful than assuming one frequency or brightness setting applies to every OLED screen.

### Use Saturation with a Clear Purpose

Strong saturation can compete with text and obscure hierarchy. For large backgrounds, we explored quieter alternatives while checking the contrast of important content.

For children's products, we worked with designers to reduce excessive saturation while preserving each app's identity and readability.

Light and dark themes should both remain readable. Following the system preference and offering a manual choice lets people adapt the interface to their surroundings.

### Account for Movement

A moving device makes small details harder to follow. For use in motion, the design guidance emphasized clear text, simpler content, and appropriate interaction targets. Any motion-triggered reminder needed a clear purpose and a gentle, optional presentation.

## Explore Warmer Display Colors

The original study also explored warmer palettes and screen-content rendering. These were interface experiments, with color, brightness, and contrast evaluated together.

The [American Academy of Ophthalmology](https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/blue-light-digital-eye-strain) reports no meaningful link between blue light from digital devices and damage to human retinas. Evening light exposure and sleep timing are separate concerns from visual comfort.

### Prototype the Color Change

Hardware filters alter the spectrum reaching the eye. Our interface work focused on how the resulting colors, brightness, and contrast affected the design.

I created a SwiftUI prototype to explore warmer color rendering on screen content. Its temperature values were prototype choices to evaluate with the content and display in use. A GPU-based implementation offered another way to adjust rendered colors.

## Outcome and Learning

The handbook gave product teams a way to discuss viewing conditions through explicit assumptions, calculations, specifications, and interface examples. Its applications included viewing-distance reminders, typography systems, pre-K interface work, and color-rendering prototypes.

My role connected research with the details teams could use in a product: what distance to evaluate, how to size text, and what to check when colors or surroundings changed.
