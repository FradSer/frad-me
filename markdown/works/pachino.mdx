Pachino is an independent product I designed and built in SwiftUI for iOS and macOS. I wanted a Pomodoro timer that felt at home on both platforms and stayed out of the way while I worked.

## Context and Role

I learn by making things. Pachino grew from a simple principle:

> Make something useful, and learn what it takes.

Rather than start with a programming language or tool, I chose a daily task I could improve. A focus timer was small enough to build independently and useful enough to keep testing through my own routine.

My work covered the timer interactions, platform-specific interfaces, generative visuals, ambient audio experience, and native implementation.

The name comes from Pachino, a tomato grown on the southeast coast of Sicily—a nod to the Pomodoro Technique.

## Keep the Timer Quiet and Predictable

The first design rule was restraint: a useful tool should be quiet until it is needed. I kept controls in predictable places and gave the countdown a clear, consistent structure.

### Show When a Session Will End

A 25-minute focus session can collide with a scheduled meeting. I added an end-time indicator so people could see when a session would finish before starting it. The indicator stays secondary to the countdown.

### Make Daily Progress Visible

Daily goals give people a simple way to recognize their progress without adding another task to manage.

## Give Breaks a Different Atmosphere

Breaks needed a different atmosphere from focus sessions. I explored generative visuals with Metal and GLSL, then used p5.js to prototype them quickly in the browser.

The resulting zen mode pairs changing graphics with a quiet time display and hides the iOS status bar for a more immersive break. It gave the visual experiments a purpose within the timer's routine.

## Share Views, Adapt the Platform Experience

I chose to build a dedicated macOS app rather than rely on Catalyst alone. Separating shared content from its platform-specific container let me reuse core views while adapting the surrounding experience to each device.

The purchase interface demonstrates that separation: the same view and code sit within different platform containers.

## Outcome and Next Direction

The result was a released focus timer for iOS and macOS, with daily goals, zen visuals, ambient audio, and a platform-specific Mac experience. The project connected interaction design and implementation through a tool I could use in my own workday.

Its central design lesson was to give each addition a clear place in the routine: timing supports focus, visuals mark a change of pace, and audio remains a choice.

I am now exploring a task-planning and focus workflow for Pachino. That exploration is separate from the released timer shown here.
