I learn by making things. Pachino grew from a simple principle:

> Make something useful, and learn what it takes.

Rather than start with a tool or programming language, I looked for a daily task I could improve. A focus timer was small enough to build independently and useful enough to keep testing.

As a regular iPhone and Mac user, I wanted a Pomodoro timer that felt at home on both platforms and stayed out of the way while I worked.

# Designing an Accurate Timer

The first design rule was restraint: a useful tool should be quiet until it is needed. Pachino keeps its controls in predictable places and gives the timer a clear, consistent structure.

The name comes from Pachino, a tomato grown on the southeast coast of Sicily—a nod to the Pomodoro Technique.

A 25-minute focus session can collide with a scheduled meeting. I added an end-time indicator so people could see when a session would finish before starting it. The indicator stays secondary to the countdown.

Breaks needed a different atmosphere from focus sessions. I explored generative visuals with Metal and GLSL, then used p5.js to prototype them quickly in the browser. The resulting zen mode pairs changing graphics with a quiet time display and hides the iOS status bar for a more immersive break.

# Designed for Each Platform

I chose to build a dedicated macOS app rather than rely on Catalyst alone. Separating shared content from its platform-specific container let me reuse the core views while adapting the surrounding experience to each device.

Ambient audio became another part of the experience. After trying options with friends, I added café, subway, ocean, rain, metronome, and seasonal sounds alongside white noise.
